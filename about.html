What the map shows

Choose Auckland, Christchurch or Wellington to load that city’s map and parking data. Each view covers the urban area inside its map bounds.

Dots show individual locations, coloured lines show street segments, and filled shapes show mapped areas. Select a feature to see what it represents and who published it. Follow posted signs and road markings when parking.

Using the map

  1. Explore a location. Click or tap a parking feature to see its source details. Where mapped features overlap, their details are listed together. Open Locations to browse the list, then select an entry to highlight it and zoom to its boundary. Use Show more to see further entries.
  2. Open it in Maps. Point-location details include a link to your device’s maps service. A recorded point may not be a car park entrance. Area details do not invent a navigation destination from a polygon centre.
  3. Find parking near you. Choose Locate me and allow location access. If you are in the selected city’s map area, the map centres on your position and the list sorts by approximate distance. Choose the button again to stop location updates.
  4. Change the background. On mobile, open Map options to find the base map and theme controls. The Base map selector offers OpenStreetMap Streets, Humanitarian and Terrain (OpenTopoMap). These backgrounds do not change the parking information.
  5. Choose parking layers. In Map options, expand Parking layers to see the selected city’s layers and record counts. Layer choices are saved per city in this browser.
  6. Choose a theme. The theme button cycles through Auto, Light and Dark. Auto follows your device’s colour preference.

What the colours mean

Scheduled parking changes colour using New Zealand local time (NZST or NZDT). The map, list and open details update every minute.

Where the information comes from

The NZ Free Parking software is licensed under the GNU General Public License, version 3 (GPLv3).

Auckland parking

The map queries Auckland Transport’s Car Parking service, linked from the data.govt.nz catalogue. It includes location points, descriptions, addresses and some space counts. The published dataset is licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0.

The map uses charging hours from the AT city-centre schedule and tariff map and the AT Takapuna schedule and tariff map. Payment IDs were matched to street sections on those maps using AT road centrelines on 21 November 2026. The charging hours and matches are maintained in the app. See AT’s city-centre parking changes for updates on overnight charging.

  • AT Paid Parking Zones — mapped zone polygons and zone IDs, without tariff schedules.
  • AT Loading Zones — polygon areas with restriction and operating-time fields.
  • Auckland Council Park Structure Hard Surface — car-park surface footprints among other parks assets. A footprint does not establish fees, access or parking rules.

AT data is credited to Auckland Transport. Car-park footprint data is credited to Auckland Council. AT also publishes separate information on mobility parking and central Auckland car parks.

Christchurch parking

Christchurch City Council publishes casual on-street parking segments and reserved on-street parking points. The map uses their published charging windows and time limits, including the council’s public-holiday meter exemption. See the council’s geospatial data terms.

Wellington parking

Wellington City Council’s on-street parking areas include metered, time-limited and reserved parking. Where the feed has complete timings, the map displays the applicable restriction. Check the signs or meter for charges and any exceptions. See Wellington City Council Open Data for data terms.

Refresh and holiday rules

The app fetches parking records within the selected city’s map bounds and refreshes that city every 15 minutes. The “Updated” time records the latest fetch attempt. If a feed fails, earlier records may remain visible. Records without drawable geometry are omitted and reported beside the list.

Holiday-sensitive hours use Employment New Zealand’s 2026 and 2027 dates, including observed days and the relevant provincial anniversary. The app applies NZTA’s parking sign rules to supported schedules.

Map display uses MapLibre GL JS for GPU-accelerated rendering. If it cannot start or its graphics context is lost, the app switches to Leaflet as a backup. The active renderer is shown below the map. The selected base map’s source credit is also shown on the map itself.

Important limits

  • Coverage is incomplete. The map shows features supplied by the selected city’s feeds. Check local signs for unmapped spaces.
  • Rule schedules are incomplete. Some records have missing, school-term or otherwise ambiguous hours. Open the details for the published information and follow signage for the specific space.
  • Space counts are not live occupancy. A listed capacity or availability figure does not guarantee an empty space when you arrive.
  • Published details can lag changes. Follow the signs and road markings at the location, including temporary restrictions.

Location and preferences

Your location is requested when you choose Locate me. The browser uses that position to centre the map and sort nearby locations. You can stop updates with the same button or change location permission in your browser settings.

Your base map, layer visibility and theme choices are saved in this browser. Loading the map and parking information makes requests to the selected data providers. Following an “Open in Maps” link sends the selected parking location to the maps service.

The public site uses Google Analytics to measure page visits. The map does not send your Locate me coordinates to Google Analytics.
